Stocktwits Sentiment Dashboard
Built by Samuel Grana  ·  Supervised by Dr. Kaamran Raahemifar  ·  Pennsylvania State University
What This Is

This dashboard monitors real-time social sentiment from Stocktwits to detect early momentum shifts in small-cap and OTC stocks. It combines crowd psychology from social media with AI financial NLP (FinBERT), official regulatory filings, and price data to give a complete, multi-source picture of market activity around any screened ticker — updated every 45 seconds.

The core insight is that Stocktwits message density and price often move together before a breakout. When the crowd starts talking about a stock at the same time the price is rising, that correlation is an early signal worth watching — even before news breaks on the wire.

How Data Flows
1
Finviz Elite
Provides the live ticker universe. Filters for high relative volume and price movement — only stocks with unusual activity enter the pipeline.
→
2
Pipeline
Fetches the latest Stocktwits messages for every ticker concurrently every 90 seconds. New tickers are backfilled with 24 hours of history on first encounter.
→
3
FinBERT AI
Each message is scored by FinBERT, a financial NLP model. Output: sentiment (−1 to +1), trust (poster credibility), and impact (event keywords + engagement).
→
4
MongoDB
All scored messages and price snapshots are stored. The score calculator runs across the rolling window and computes the correlation signal on each refresh.
→
5
Dashboard
Tickers are ranked and rendered live — screener table, correlation cards, news cards, bubble map, and exit monitor all pull from the same scored dataset.
Main Dashboard

The main table is the starting point. Every ticker returned by your Finviz screener appears here, ranked by composite sentiment score descending. The top three headline numbers — Top Bullish, Top Bearish, Most Active — update every refresh cycle and give you an instant summary of where the crowd's attention is concentrated.

Columns — Price and % Change come from Finviz — pulled directly from the same screener export that supplies the ticker list. Rolling Score, Bull %, Bear %, Scored Msgs, and Total Msgs all come from Stocktwits message data scored by FinBERT. Msg Density shows how many messages per hour are being posted. CORR is the correlation between message density and price over the last 30 minutes — a positive high value means crowd activity and price are moving together.

Sorting — Click any column header to re-sort. Click again to reverse. Sort by correlation, price change, density, or composite score depending on what you are hunting for.

Filter Bar — Narrow the table instantly by Change direction (Up only / Down only), Relative Volume minimum, Average Volume minimum, Market Cap, minimum Score, minimum Price, or a text search by ticker or company name. All filters apply without a reload. Use Reset Filters to clear everything.

Time Window — The window buttons (15m · 30m · 1h · 4h · 1d · 1w) control how far back all score calculations look. Shorter windows react faster to breaking moves; longer windows filter noise. The window applies globally to the table, popup charts, and news cards.

Per-row density and sentiment windows — Each row has its own rolling window selector for Msg Density and Msg Sentiment. These let you compare, for example, sentiment over the last hour while density is measured over the last 5 minutes — useful for spotting a spike in posting activity even when the overall hourly score is flat.

Ticker Popup

Click any row to open the detail popup for that ticker. The popup overlays a combined chart, live score cards, scored messages, and recent news — all for the currently selected window.

Combined Chart — Price (red line), Rolling Sentiment Score (blue dashed), and Message Density (green fill) are all normalized to a 0–1 axis so they can be compared on the same chart regardless of their different units. Hover any point to see the raw values in the tooltip. Switch the chart window using the row of buttons at the top of the popup — this does not change the global window.

Event Markers — Colored triangle markers appear on the chart at the exact time a keyword event was detected in Stocktwits messages. Each color maps to an event type (FDA, earnings, squeeze, merger, clinical trial). Hover the triangle to read the event text extracted from the original message.

Entry Triangle — If you have an active monitored position for this ticker (marked via the Correlation view), a green ▲ triangle appears on the price chart at your entry time and price. When viewing completed trades from the trade history, both a green ▲ entry and a red ▼ exit marker are plotted.

Technical Indicators — Toggle SMA overlays (SMA 5, 10, 20) and MACD using the indicator buttons above the chart. These are computed from the price series currently loaded in the chart window.

Score Cards — Below the chart, four live score values update every refresh: Composite, Sentiment, Trust, and Impact. The scored message count tells you how many messages were processed vs. how many total messages exist in the window.

Navigation — When opened from the Correlation view, left and right arrow buttons let you flip through all tickers in that view without closing the popup. Use the ⊙ Map button to jump directly to the Bubble Map for this ticker; closing the bubble map returns you to the popup.

Score System

Every Stocktwits message produces three sub-scores that combine into a single composite ranking signal.

Sentiment
Raw FinBERT output — how bullish or bearish the message language is. Range: −1 to +1. Positive means bullish. A message like "FDA approval is going to send this to the moon" scores strongly positive.
Trust
Weighted by the poster's follower count, ideas published, and account classification. An Official or verified account with 10,000 followers is weighted far more than an anonymous account with 3 followers.
Impact
Boosted when the message contains a catalyst keyword (FDA, earnings, merger, squeeze, etc.) or when the stock symbol appears in an unusual number of posts simultaneously, indicating a coordinated crowd reaction.
Composite
The weighted average of Sentiment × Trust × Impact across all scored messages in the selected window. This is the primary ranking signal — the number that determines where a ticker sits in the main table.
Event Tags — Extracted From Stocktwits Messages

Event tags are automatically detected from the text of Stocktwits messages. When a user writes "$BCRX FDA approval imminent", the system extracts the keyword and classifies the message. Tags appear as colored badges on ticker rows in the main table, and as triangle markers on the popup chart at the exact minute the event language appeared.

FDA Approval / Rejection / PDUFA Earnings Beat / Miss / EPS Merger / Acquisition / M&A Clinical Trial Results Short Squeeze
Correlation View

The Correlation view is the primary signal tool. It measures the correlation between message density and price over a 30-minute rolling lookback for every screener ticker. A high positive correlation — say 80% — means that as more people posted about this stock, the price was moving up at the same rate. That alignment between crowd activity and price is the earliest detectable signal of a momentum trade setting up.

Reading a correlation card — The large percentage is the correlation score. The trend arrow (↑ Rising / → Stable / ↓ Falling) shows whether that correlation has been strengthening or weakening since the last refresh. Below the score you see message density, bull percentage, time in the current section, and the current price. Negative correlation always displays as ↓ Falling regardless of history, since it means density and price are moving in opposite directions.

Sections — Tickers are distributed across six sections automatically based on their correlation score and price/density direction:

Entry Zone
Correlation at or above your threshold AND both price and density are rising. These are the highest-conviction setups — the crowd and the price agree.
Watching
Positive correlation below the entry threshold, or price and density not yet both rising. Worth monitoring — may move to Entry Zone on the next refresh.
My Watch
User-pinned section. Drag any card here to keep it visible regardless of its auto-assigned section or message count filter.
High Priority
User-pinned section for tickers you are actively tracking as trade candidates.
Researching
User-pinned section for tickers under longer-term research before a decision.
Ignored
User-pinned section to suppress tickers you have evaluated and decided to pass on.

Drag and drop — Drag any card between sections to override its auto-assignment. A pinned badge appears on the card. Click ✕ pinned to release it back to automatic sorting. Positions are saved in localStorage and persist across page refreshes.

Ticker search — Type any ticker symbol in the search box at the top of the Correlation view. Matching tickers appear in a dropdown. Click to add a ticker directly to My Watch — useful for tracking a specific stock that did not make it through the auto-filter.

Entry threshold — The slider at the bottom of the Correlation view sets the minimum correlation % required to appear in Entry Zone. Lower it to widen the signal pool; raise it to show only the strongest setups.

News View

Switch to News View to see all screener tickers as cards, each with a mini price chart, composite score, recent Stocktwits messages, and recent news articles. News View shows only tickers above the message density threshold — the ones with genuine crowd activity right now.

The news articles are not the source of the sentiment score — they exist to confirm what Stocktwits users are already talking about. If the crowd is bullish and a legitimate press release just went out, that agreement between social sentiment and official news gives you a more complete picture of what is driving activity. If the crowd is buzzing but no news exists, that is often the early signal — the crowd is reacting to something before it hits the wire.

All news sources carry an editorial or regulatory standard that was a condition of inclusion:

SEC 8-K
The most authoritative source. Legally required SEC filings for any material event — companies must file within four business days of a significant announcement. Fetched directly from EDGAR. No date cutoff.
PR Newswire
Regulated paid wire service. Companies verify identity and take legal responsibility for accuracy. Fetched via Google News scoped to prnewswire.com.
Business Wire
Berkshire Hathaway-owned paid press release service used by public companies for official announcements. Fetched via Google News.
GlobeNewswire
Nasdaq's official wire service. Companies listed on Nasdaq use GlobeNewswire as their primary distribution channel. Fetched via direct RSS by ticker keyword.
Benzinga
Professional financial news covering analyst ratings, earnings previews, and market-moving stories. Fetched via Finnhub API — only Benzinga articles, no tabloid sources.
FDA
FDA-related news detected from Finviz Stocks News. Shows upcoming PDUFA decision dates and approval or rejection events for biotech and pharma tickers.
Signals Panel — Live Message Stream

Click Signals in the top bar to open the side panel. It streams the most recently scored Stocktwits messages across all active screener tickers in real time — the individual posts that received the highest composite scores in the current window. Use it to read exactly what the crowd is saying without opening each ticker popup individually.

Messages are sorted by composite score descending. Each entry shows the ticker, the raw message text, the FinBERT sentiment label (bullish / bearish / neutral), and the time posted. A spike in high-scoring messages for a single ticker in this panel is often the first visible sign of a developing setup before the correlation or score columns update.

You can filter the panel by ticker using the search box. Click any message row to open the full ticker popup for that ticker.

Exit Monitor — Position Tracking

The Exit Monitor tracks active positions you have marked from the Correlation view. When you see a ticker in the Entry Zone that you decide to act on, click Enter Trade on its correlation card and enter your entry price. The system records your entry time, entry correlation %, and entry price, and begins tracking that ticker's message density peak from that moment forward.

Density falloff signal — After entry, message density almost always rises as crowd interest peaks. The Exit Monitor tracks the highest density that ticker reached after your entry (the peak). As density falls from that peak, a progress bar fills. When density has fallen a significant percentage from its peak, the bar turns red — signaling that the crowd is leaving and momentum may be exhausting. This is the exit cue.

Bull % on exit — The monitor also shows the current bull percentage next to each position. A falling bull % alongside falling density is a stronger exit signal than density alone.

Entry marker on chart — When you open the popup for a ticker you are actively monitoring, a green ▲ triangle appears on the price chart at your exact entry time and price. When you view a completed trade from the trade history, both the green ▲ entry and the red ▼ exit are plotted on the chart so you can review the full trade in context.

Exiting a position — Click Exit on the monitor card to close the position. The trade is saved to history with your entry and exit times and the price at exit. Click View Chart on any completed trade to reopen the popup with entry and exit markers plotted.

Bubble Map

The Bubble Map gives you a visual overview of the entire screener universe at once. Each bubble represents one ticker — the horizontal position encodes sentiment score, the vertical position encodes price change, and the bubble size encodes message volume. Color shifts from red (bearish) through yellow (neutral) to green (bullish) based on the composite score.

Time slider — Scrub the slider at the bottom to replay how the sentiment landscape shifted across the trading day. This lets you visually trace when a ticker's crowd activity and sentiment aligned — and how long that alignment lasted.

Solo mode — Click any bubble to isolate that ticker. The view zooms to show just that stock's bubble moving through the space over time. Click the ⊙ button on a Correlation popup to jump directly to that ticker's solo view in the Bubble Map and return to the popup when done.

Filters — The Bubble Map filter modal lets you show only tickers above a minimum correlation, minimum density, or within a score range. Use this to cut the visual noise and focus on only the strongest setups.

PDUFA & Earnings Catalysts

The dashboard tracks upcoming FDA decision deadlines (PDUFA dates) from the BioPharma Catalyst database and upcoming earnings dates from Finviz. These appear as countdown badges on ticker rows and on News View cards. Catalysts often explain sudden spikes in Stocktwits activity days or weeks before the official announcement — the crowd finds out early, and the sentiment data reflects it.

Screener Performance & Recommended Filters

The number of tickers your Finviz screener returns directly controls how fast the pipeline loops. The pipeline fetches messages concurrently but is still rate-limited. With too many tickers, the loop takes several minutes and data grows stale between cycles.

50–150 tickers
Loop time ~15–45 seconds. Near real-time updates. Best range for active monitoring during market hours.
200–500 tickers
Loop time 1–3 minutes. Sentiment updates lag slightly but are still useful for research and swing setups.
500+ tickers
Loop time 3–10+ minutes. Data grows stale between cycles. Tighten screener filters to reduce the universe.

Recommended starting filters — these four together typically return 30–100 tickers and keep loop time under 30 seconds:

Change: Up only
Limits the universe to stocks moving upward. Focuses the dashboard on tickers where positive sentiment is most actionable.
Rel Vol ≥ 3×
3× relative volume means a stock is trading at triple its normal rate — a strong indicator of unusual activity. Below 2× lets in too many routine names.
Cur Vol ≥ 500k
Ensures enough real market participation. Tickers below this threshold often have thin Stocktwits activity and less reliable sentiment data.
Price ≥ $1.00
Filters out sub-penny stocks, which tend to have artificially inflated Stocktwits activity that does not reflect genuine market sentiment.

Adjust Rel Vol up or down to widen or narrow the universe. Raise it to 5× or higher during slow market sessions to cut noise; lower it to 2× during high-activity sessions to catch more names.
